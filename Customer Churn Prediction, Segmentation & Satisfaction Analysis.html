This is because the model needs to be good at predicting both churning and not churning so that we can know what factors cause customers to churn and what factors influence customers to not churn.

Since Accuracy and ROC measure the model’s predictability on both positive and negative classes, these two will be relevant for evaluating the model’s performance. As for Sensitivity and Specificity, they only measure the predictability of only one between positive or negative classes, they are not preferred in this case.

Determine the number of clusters

Elbow method: When plotting the WCSS with the K value, the plot will appear like an Elbow. As the number of clusters increases, the WCSS value will start to decrease. WCSS value is the largest when k = 1. The graph will show a rapid change at a point and thus creating an elbow shape. From this point, the graph starts to move almost parallel to the X-axis. The K value corresponding to this point is the optimal K value or an optimal number of clusters. Based on the plot above, the elbow is created at a sharp fall of average distance at k=2, 3, and 4. With this method, it is hard to identify which k value is the best.

Silhouette method: The Silhouette method is also a method to find the optimal number of clusters and interpretation and validation of consistency within clusters of data. This method computes silhouette coefficients of each point that measure how much a point is similar to its own cluster compared to other clusters by providing a succinct graphical representation of how well each object has been classified. For each k-Means clustering model represents the silhouette coefficients in a plot and observe the fluctuations and outliers of each cluster. Based on the plot above, k=3 is said to be the best value as it has the highest average silhouette width. Silhouette method is better compared to the elbow method when finding optimal clusters because it is more obvious. Therefore, the number of clusters chosen is 3.

Describing the components

#Description for PC1

The variables of the 3 highest loadings shown in PC1 are x2 (Competitive Prices), x3 (Competent Employees), and x6 (Fast Service). The graph above shows that PC1 at x2 and x6 are highly correlated but at x3 is lowly correlated. The negative loadings for competent employees but positive loadings for competitive prices and fast service indicates that a higher PC1 represents low satisfaction towards competent employees but high satisfaction towards competitive prices and fast service. Hence, PC1 represents good competitive prices and satisfactory fast service but lack of competent employees.

#Description for PC2

The variables of the 3 highest loadings shown in PC2 are x1 (Friendly Employees), x4 (Excellent Food Quality), and x5 (Wide variety of food). All the high loadings for PC2 are represented by variables that deal with customer’s ratings towards the restaurant. As the loading values of all the three main variables for PC2 are positive, a higher PC2 represents a higher customer rating towards the restaurant’s employee friendliness, food quality and wide variety of foods. Hence, high PC2 represents the employees are friendly, followed by the food having high quality and wide variety.
